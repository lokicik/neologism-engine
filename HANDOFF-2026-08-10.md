## Read this first

The product is a browser-first, offline naming engine. Its most important use case is helping the
owner name their own developer projects. The current priority is **better names without a runtime
LLM**, not merely higher structural scores.

The important product split is:

- Auto is the strongest default offline path.
- Explicit Brandable, Real words, Respelled, and Compound modes remain exploration tools.
- AI Studio is an optional, separate batch judge. It must not become a hidden dependency of Create.
- Developer naming is the positioning advantage: npm/crates.io-style namespace checks, package
  naming, and eventually Wordoid-like language accents.

The user’s core complaint remains valid: many names are technically readable and relevant but do
not feel beautiful enough to choose. Treat “would I actually name my project this?” as the real
outcome. Do not optimize only for the current pronounceability/novelty/memorability composite.

## What Phase 140 discovered

### 1. Higher structural score can mean worse real quality

A recruiter prototype forced `HireHub` into the visible page. The numerical A/B looked positive:
global average rose slightly, recruiter average improved, and similarity improved. Live usage checks
then showed that HireHub is already used by several recruiting products in the exact same domain.
The prototype was metric gaming and was fully reverted.

Examples checked during that investigation:

- https://www.hirehub.tech/
- https://hirehub.cloud/
- https://www.hiremap.co.in/
- https://jobset.io/fr
- https://crewset.app/
- https://talentmap.com/

Other apparently strong literal pairs (`HireArc`, `TalentArc`, `JobArc`, `CrewArc`, `Hirebeam`,
`CrewSeed`, `TalentLab`) were also already occupied. Do not solve weak pages by hardcoding another
generic domain-root + `Hub/Map/Set/Arc/Lab` pair without real collision review.

### 2. Raw-root blending is not the next generator family

A diagnostic bypassed semantic expansion and blended raw recruiter roots. It produced candidates
such as `Trarly`, `Trashia`, `Recred`, `Candarn`, and `Candass`, some with structural scores above
90. That experiment was rejected and removed from the current diagnostic. It proves the scorer can
strongly overvalue short, pronounceable nonsense. Do not ship raw-root coinage as a quality fix.

### 3. Hidden join seams are a real but narrow aesthetic defect

The held-out visible output contained `Busharbor`, produced by `bus + harbor`. The engine interprets
the intended roots correctly, but a reader naturally reparses it as “bush arbor.”

Two approaches were tested:

1. Rejecting `sh/ch/th/ph/wh` join seams inside `metaphor_join` removed the bad form but changed the
   deterministic RNG stream. Several unrelated pages regressed and five held-out gates failed. That
   version was reverted.
2. The current uncommitted `core/src/lib.rs` keeps candidates in the pool and applies a `0.50` rank
   penalty only when a prompt root plus the next character forms a dictionary word across one of
   those digraph seams. It includes a focused unit test.

The second approach passes **160/160 Rust tests** and restores nearly every Phase 139 metric:

- average **84.85**
- lead **88.22**
- coverage **1.40**
- near pairs **62**
- similarity **0.197**
- suffix leads **6**
- guided leads **84**
- seed diversity **19.57/30**
- seed overlap **4.42/10**

However, it is still **not shippable**. The full held-out audit fails exactly the two message-queue
contracts:

- message-queue diversity becomes **25/30**, below the retained minimum of 26.
- average seed overlap becomes **2.00**, above the retained maximum of 1.70.
- The same failure repeats for the three wording variants because those pages intentionally stay
  wording-stable.

`Busharbor` disappears, but seed 67 fills the slot with `Pipeora`, which already appears in another
seed. This is why a sensible local penalty can still lower set quality.

Do not commit the current `core/src/lib.rs` experiment unless those failures are resolved without
metric gaming. Reverting the experiment to Phase 139 is preferable to weakening the gates.

## Honest quality assessment

The current engine is strong at:

- staying semantically tied to a brief;
- avoiding obvious junk and damaged Respells in Auto;
- producing ten usable, diverse, deterministic candidates;
- handling developer-product vocabulary;
- maintaining long offline sessions without repeats.

It is still weak at:

- distinguishing a technically valid string from a name a human genuinely loves;
- avoiding cliché root + suffix/metaphor families across an entire page;
- detecting same-domain brand collisions without a live search;
- understanding subtle lexical reparsing and cultural/semantic associations;
- generating holistic Wordoid-style coinages instead of visible root assembly.

The current app-naming output illustrates the ceiling. A representative own-product page included:

`Keyloom, Lexify, LexMint, Keyscope, Markio, Nomion, Markalias, Mintalias, Nomify, Lexora`.

These are structurally clean and relevant, but most still feel assembled rather than inevitable.

## Recommended continuation order

1. **Reconfirm the worktree.** Preserve the six user-owned Rust files.
2. **Resolve or revert the seam prototype.** Do not weaken message-queue gates. If no general
   quality-preserving replacement rule emerges, remove the `core/src/lib.rs` experiment and keep
   Phase 139 production behavior.
3. **Keep measurement honest.** A higher composite is not sufficient. Add diagnostics for visible
   lexical hazards and construction saturation before changing ranking globally.
4. **Use real preference evidence.** The product already exports versioned local taste data and has
   `core/examples/taste_audit.rs`. Collect at least 10 liked and 10 passed names in matching project
   contexts before changing broad aesthetic weights. Pairwise held-out agreement is the acceptance
   criterion.
5. **Prototype the next generator family separately.** The promising LLM-free expansion is a
   syllable/phonotactic, language-accented lane inspired by Wordoid, evaluated beside production
   Auto before integration. Do not replace the proven generator or reuse the failed raw-root blend.
6. **Treat availability as utility, not beauty.** npm/crates/domain checks can improve usefulness,
   but they do not make an ugly name good and cannot detect every same-domain product collision.
